## Canonical field-note draft

# I built Woon and waited

Three years ago, I built an iMessage agent called Woon.

This was before most people were using agents in ordinary conversation. I do not think the underlying idea was mine alone, but it was real enough to build and early enough that relatively few people had seen it expressed as a product.

I believed the hard part was making it exist.

If the product worked and the idea mattered, I assumed the right people would eventually find it. "Build it and they will come" was not a slogan I consciously repeated. It was simply the model I was operating with.

They did not come.

Maybe Woon was too early. Maybe the product was not good enough. I still do not have a clean answer. But I can see one failure much more clearly now: I did not know how to put the idea into circulation.

I did not know where people were already discussing the problem. I did not know how to find the people who would immediately understand what an iMessage agent could become. I did not know how to express the idea in a form that could move through those networks, gather criticism, and return sharper than it left.

I had confused building the thing with giving the thing a chance.

Those are separate capabilities.

Some people are naturally fluent in networks. They reply quickly. They know which conversation matters, how to reach someone, how to make an idea legible, and how to keep it moving without losing the thread.

I am not naturally that kind of person.

For a long time I treated that as a personal limitation. Now I see it as a product problem.

What if Woon had arrived with an agent for its own participation? It could have found the small pockets of people already building conversational interfaces, translated the product into the language of each conversation, and shown me where my lived point of view actually added something. It could have helped me place the idea in front of the people capable of shaping it.

It should not have pretended to be me. It should not have manufactured relationships or sprayed automated replies across the internet. The final expression still needed to be mine.

But it could have made the network legible. It could have given the idea a megaphone I knew how to hold.

That is part of why I am building Agents for Introverts.

The goal is not to make people post more. It is to help people with something real to contribute enter the discourse without having to live inside the feed. Agents can find recurrence, preserve disagreement, retrieve what you already believe, and help your experience travel. You decide what is true, what is new, and what leaves the room.

Woon may still have failed. But I would rather have learned that after it met its network.

What did you build that never found the people who could understand or shape it?
