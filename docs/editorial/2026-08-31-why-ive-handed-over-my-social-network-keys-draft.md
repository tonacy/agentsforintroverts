# Why I've handed over all my social network keys to my agents

*I want to participate more without living inside the feed.*

When I told my agent the title of this essay, it tried to protect me from it.

It worried that “handing over the keys” sounded like I had given an AI every password I own. I told it that it was overreacting. Handing over the keys is a familiar saying. I still own the car. I choose where we are going. I can take the keys back. The agent is doing more of the driving.

That exchange is a decent example of the relationship I am trying to build. My agents can catch risks, retrieve context, and help me put an idea into the world. They can also misunderstand what I mean. I correct them. Over time, we find the line between what they can handle and what still requires me.

When I started writing this, none of the keys had changed hands. I wanted to publish only after that sentence stopped being true.

The transfer happens one permission at a time.

I am handing them more of the driving because the way I use social networks has stopped working for me.

X is the clearest example. Nearly every day, I enter with one clear intention and find myself somewhere I never meant to go. It is both a place where I need to publish and the feed most likely to pull me away from what I came there to do. The composer and the current live behind the same door.

My instinct is to disengage. That would protect my attention, but it would also repeat a mistake I have made before.

A few years ago, I built Woon, an agent that lived inside iMessage before that kind of product was familiar. I believed the idea needed to exist, so I built it. Then, more or less, I waited.

I had the “build it and they will come” mentality. I knew how to make the product. I did not know how to find the people who might understand it, put it in front of them, or explain it in a form they could carry to somebody else.

Maybe Woon was too early. Maybe it would have failed anyway. I still do not know. I know it never reached the network of people who could have challenged it, shaped it, used it, or helped it travel. My lack of network fluency was part of its failure.

Network fluency is a real skill. It means replying quickly, knowing how to navigate toward the right person, making an idea engaging without flattening it, and turning it into something that can move across a network. Some people speak that language naturally. I do not.

I no longer think I can pretend it does not matter.

As AI takes on more of the work we once used to prove what we could do, I think relationship will become the scarce currency. Capability will still matter. The people who know you, understand your work, know what you care about, and trust you enough to make something happen with you will matter more.

Many of those relationships now begin online. An idea that never enters the network has a hard time finding the people who might care about it.

The feed makes me want to disengage at the exact moment participation matters more than ever. Agents for Introverts is my attempt to work through that contradiction.

## The feed is an interface for a network

The feeds never stop because the network never stops.

Hundreds of people can be having versions of the same conversation in different pockets at once. They repeat one another, disagree by degrees, and continuously create more context than one person can absorb. A feed presents all of that activity as a stream of individual posts. It serves the network’s output while leaving each person to reconstruct the understanding.

I want my agents to turn that stream back into something I can use.

Four hundred posts might contain one recurring conversation, several real disagreements, and a small number of people whose work is directly relevant to mine. I need to see what is repeating, where the apparent agreement breaks down, why any of it matters to what I am doing, and how to return to the original sources when I want to challenge the compression.

That is the incoming side of the job. The agent acts as a lens.

The outgoing side matters just as much. My projects, prior ideas, corrections, and established positions should not disappear every time I close a tab. When one of those ideas belongs in a live conversation, an agent should be able to find the opening, retrieve what I have already said, and shape it for the place where the conversation is happening.

The agent acts as a megaphone by making an idea available where it can meet another person. More volume would defeat the purpose.

## What the keys open

I want to have one conversation with my agents each day.

They bring a bounded view of what is happening outside: recurring conversations, meaningful disagreement, work that intersects with my current priorities, and anything that came back from places we entered before. I bring the texture of my day: what I worked on, what changed, what I care about making happen, and how much energy I have.

Together we look for zero to three places where those two sides genuinely meet. A place might be a particular post, an exchange, a person, a publication, or a project where I have something real to add. Zero is a good result when nothing has earned my attention.

When something does fit, the agent can research it, recover my established position, prepare a reply or a longer piece, adapt it for the channel, and eventually publish within rules I have set.

Those rules will be different on every surface.

My intended end state on X is broad standing permission. Once an idea has been released for public use and the agent understands the boundaries, I do not want to approve every individual post or reply. I want to be able to publish without entering the feed, and I want the agent to participate in conversations where my established ideas genuinely belong.

Substack will be more deliberate. Every essay and subscriber send will come back to me. LinkedIn will have its own rules. Original posts, replies, private messages, follows, deletions, and anything that commits money or my time are separate permissions.

The keys are a set of specific permissions that I can narrow, pause, or take back. If an agent misrepresents a belief, exposes private context, makes a promise, or starts producing noise for its own sake, I take the relevant keys back.

The software is early, and the first month will be more cautious than that end state. The agents can already help me retrieve context, structure ideas, research, and draft. At the start of this experiment, publication was manual on every channel. The first handoff above changes only the permission named there. Everything else remains manual until it earns a different rule.

The product is that negotiation. Every correction helps define where an agent can gain more agency and where I need to return.

## What stays with me

My agents should know more about me over time as a growing context I manage with them. A synthetic persona performing everywhere would break the point.

I think of the system as two separate contexts.

Inside is where private work, lived experience, relationships, reflection, and ideas that are still forming belong. Outside contains public sources, public conversations, and ideas I have deliberately released. The two sides meet during reflection, with me in the middle.

I decide when something crosses. Outside receives only the released idea. The private record stays Inside.

An agent can carry an established position. A new belief, a changed view, a vulnerable story, a promise, or a commitment of my time comes back to me. The same is true when an online exchange becomes a real relationship. The agent may find the opening and preserve the thread. The two people decide whether to continue.

This essay is already part of that practice. Its ideas came from a long conversation about my failures, my relationship with feeds, and the kind of participation I want. My agent recovered those positions and assembled the first draft. I am deciding where it is wrong, what is too polished, what is missing, and what I am willing to put my name behind.

The beliefs are mine. The agent’s work is retrieval, structure, compression, and adaptation. When this is published, I still own every sentence and every consequence that follows from it.

## The ship

The keys describe the handoff. The ship describes the larger thing I want to build.

I feel swept away by the currents of my social feeds. I have been trying to swim through them myself, but the water has become torrential. Getting out often feels like the only way to keep my attention.

I suspect I am not alone. Agents for Introverts is the ship I think we need. It should let us remain part of the outside world without asking us to absorb the network at network scale. The destination is a small number of real conversations with people I would otherwise never find.

## The first month

For the next thirty days, I am going to treat Agents for Introverts as a public practice before I treat it as a finished product.

Once a week, the agents will find one recurring conversation, preserve the disagreement inside it, and help me add my lived position. We will participate where that conversation is already happening and follow no more than three promising human threads.

I am setting a ceiling of two hours of human feed time per week. I will track how often I correct the agents, how much energy the process costs or returns, whether the contributions still feel like mine, and whether any conversation becomes a continuing relationship or shared action. There should be zero unapproved posts, messages, promises, or commitments while the initial approval boundary is in place.

More output would be an easy result and a bad one. I want to spend less time inside feeds while becoming more present in the conversations that shape my work. One real human continuation would matter more than a month of higher impressions.

This is the first note in that experiment.

If your feeds keep pulling you away from where you meant to go, reply. Tell me which part you would hand to an agent and which decisions you would keep.
