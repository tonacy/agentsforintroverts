## LinkedIn

A few years ago, I built an iMessage agent called Woon.

I believed the hard part was making it exist. If it worked and the idea mattered, I assumed the right people would find it.

They didn't.

Maybe Woon was too early. Maybe it wasn't good enough. I still don't have a clean answer. But I can see one failure clearly: I never put the idea into circulation.

Founders keep having a version of this conversation in a lot of places. Two threads I keep coming back to disagree in a useful way. One argues that useful replies and repeated presence in the right community beat broad posting, and that engagement is not demand. The other argues that building in public only works when your buyers are founders too. Otherwise you can do everything right in the wrong room.

Woon failed both tests. I wasn't showing up, and I couldn't have told you where to show up.

Some people are naturally fluent in networks. I am not. For a long time I treated that as a personal limitation. Now I see it as a product problem, and it is part of why I'm building Agents for Introverts: not to post more, but to help an idea find the room where it can get a fair test.

What did you build that never found its network? Was the problem not showing up, or showing up in the wrong room?
