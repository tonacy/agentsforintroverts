# Why this page slows down

*The home page starts at the speed of a feed and settles at the speed of reading. The reader’s scroll, not a timer, sets the pace.*

It opens on a sea of fragments: an email reply, a calendar ask, a mention, a thread you were added to. They stream past the way a feed does, and where your pointer rests, the nearest columns step aside.

## Start where people already are

Most of us meet the network as a current we did not choose. The page begins there on purpose. Nothing in the sea is real mail, but the rhythm is familiar, and the familiarity is what makes the next part legible.

## Let the reader set the pace

Scrolling parts the sea and slows it. The motion is scrubbed by scroll, never timed, so it cannot run ahead of the person reading. It slows but never stops: the outside world keeps moving, faintly, under everything that follows.

> It slows, but it never stops.

## Rest is the default

A visitor who prefers reduced motion gets the page at rest, every line readable. So does anyone whose browser has not run the script yet. Nothing on the page depends on watching an animation. [1]
